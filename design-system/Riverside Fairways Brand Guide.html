Section one

Logo

The logo pairs an elegant script wordmark with an integrated golf flag. The script carries the sophistication; the flag carries the sport. Never redraw either — always place an approved file.

Reversed · on green
Ivory · on dark green
Flag icon

Monochrome · print

Clear space & size

  • Clear space equals the height of the golf flag, on all sides.
  • Minimum width: 120px digital, 0.75in print.
  • Flag icon alone: 120px minimum — avatars, favicons, app icons.

Never

  • Stretch, compress or rotate the mark
  • Recolor it or add shadows, gradients or outlines
  • Place it on busy or low-contrast backgrounds
  • Set the wordmark in a substitute typeface

Do

  • Follow the hierarchy
  • Keep body text at 12px or larger

Don't

  • Set paragraphs in all caps
  • Justify text or distort letterforms

Section four

Layout & components

Spacing

Everything is a multiple of eight: 8 · 16 · 32 · 64 · 96 · 128. Sections breathe at 96px vertical, 64px when tight. Container 1200px; prose narrows to 760px. Minimum tap target 44px.

Corners & borders

6px on controls, 12px on cards, 20px on large panels, pill on chips. Borders are hairline — 1px Light Gray on white, 1px Warm Neutral on ivory. Section headings in documents sit above a 3px green rule.

Buttons

Get a quote See packages Disabled

Hover lightens to #7BA691; press darkens to #3D5D52. Nothing scales or bounces. On green fills and photography, use the white “reversed” button — a green button disappears.

Cards

Package
The Clubhouse

12px radius, hairline border, a green-tinted whisper of a shadow. Cards never match their section background.

Page rhythm

One hero per page, at the top. Sections alternate white and ivory going down; at most one dark-green band before the footer. The footer is always dark green with the ivory logo. Headers are transparent over a hero, solid white everywhere else.

Motion. 200ms color and background transitions on a standard ease; 120ms on small controls. Fades and color changes only — no bounce, no parallax, no entrance animations. Focus rings are never removed.

Section five

Voice & messaging

Friendly and approachable. Luxury and sophisticated. Casual and conversational — all three at once. Polished sentences delivered like a person talking.

How we write

  • “You” and “we.” Contractions always.
  • Active voice — “we'll set up your simulator.”
  • Lead with the differentiator: fully mobile, anywhere with electricity.
  • Be specific: “14 additional games,” not “fun entertainment.”
  • Paragraphs of three or four sentences, max.
  • Oxford comma. Numbers one to nine spelled out.

Primary messages

  • Fully mobile — no golf course required.
  • Premium experience — championship-quality, professionally run.
  • Any event — corporate, wedding, birthday, charity.
  • No experience needed — fun at every skill level.

Calls to action

“Reserve your simulator today” · “Plan your perfect event” · “Get a quote now” · “Schedule a demo”

Write this

“We're fully mobile — we set up anywhere you've got electricity.”

“No golf experience required. That's half the fun.”

“Tell us the date and the venue. We'll handle the rest.”

Not this

“Your simulator will be set up by our team.”

“Leverage our turnkey entertainment solution.”

“DON'T MISS OUT!!!”

Off limits

Corporate jargon, passive voice, negative framing, assumptions about golf knowledge, politics of any kind — and any unverified claim about licensing, reviews or years in business. Emoji stay on social media; never in an interface, email or print. Say “golf simulator,” never “golf video game.” The tagline is Bringing the fairway to you, never reworded.

Section six

Photography & iconography

Photography

Professional, polished, people-forward. Close-up action or medium group shots. Bright, even lighting on faces. Controlled backgrounds. Natural saturation with a slight warm cast.

Do

  • Real guests, genuine reactions
  • Warm, inviting tones
  • Faces large enough to read

Don't

  • Obvious stock, dark or moody lighting
  • Cluttered backgrounds, competing logos
  • Wide shots with tiny people

The green overlay

Any photograph carrying text gets Primary Green at 40% plus a bottom scrim. That green wash is the brand's most recognizable composition — white text never sits on a bare photo.

Iconography

The golf flag from the logo is the brand's only true glyph — a list bullet, an avatar, an empty-state mark. It is cropped from the logo artwork, never redrawn.

No icon font, sprite or icon set has ever been defined for this brand. If a utility set is genuinely needed, use a simple line set at 1.5px stroke and flag the substitution — it is not a brand asset. Emoji are copy, never interface.
